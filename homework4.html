Homework 4 - Penetration process: LLN and CLT with n systems and m attackers

Random walk with i.i.d. increments X1, X2, ... as a model of the penetration score. Everything below is simulated in your browser.

1. The model

  • Systems and attackers. There are n systems. An attacker goes through them one after the other and Xi is the penetration score obtained on the i-th system. The m attackers are independent, so each one gives one sample trajectory S1, S2, ..., Sn of the same process.
  • Bernoulli model. Xi ∈ {0,1}, P(Xi=1)=p: the system is penetrated or not. μ=p, σ²=p(1-p). Sn is the number of penetrated systems.
  • Rademacher model. Xi ∈ {-1,+1} with probability 1/2 each: the symmetric case where the attacker gains or loses ground with the same probability. μ=0, σ²=1.
  • Three quantities. Total score Sn=ΣXi, average score Sn/n, normalized total score Sn/√n. In general E[Sn]=nμ and Var(Sn)=nσ², so Var(Sn/n)=σ²/n and Var(Sn/√n)=σ²: only the last one has a variance that neither explodes nor vanishes.

6. Interpretation

  • Law of Large Numbers (Sn/n). The average score of each attacker converges in probability to μ. By Chebyshev, P(|Sn/n-μ|>ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²), which goes to 0 as 1/n. The simulated fraction lies below that bound (the bound is not tight: the true decay is exponential, by Hoeffding). With many systems, the success rate of any attacker is essentially deterministic: luck averages out.
  • Variance under the three normalizations. Sn has variance growing like n (its typical size is √n in the Rademacher case, so the total score drifts away from 0 but much more slowly than n); Sn/n has variance σ²/n → 0 (the trajectories collapse on μ); Sn/√n keeps variance σ² for every n. √n is the only scaling that "sees" the fluctuations without making them disappear or blow up.
  • Central Limit Theorem (Rademacher). Sn/√n converges in distribution to N(0,1). The histograms become bell-shaped and the Kolmogorov distance decreases like 1/√n until it hits the noise floor of about 1/√m, due to using only m trajectories. For small n the distribution is lattice-valued (Sn has the parity of n), which is why the first panels look comb-like.
  • Bernoulli with p ≠ 1/2. Here Sn/√n has mean p√n, which diverges: it needs centering. The standardized version (Sn-np)/√(np(1-p)) converges to N(0,1) too. This shows why the Rademacher case is the clean one: symmetry makes μ=0, so no centering is needed.
  • Effect of the number of systems n. Larger n means a larger total score (order nμ), a more predictable average (error σ/√n) and a spread of the total of order σ√n: the absolute uncertainty grows but the relative one, σ/(μ√n), shrinks. A defender with many identical systems can therefore forecast the aggregate penetration rate well, but not the exact number of compromised systems.
  • Effect of the number of attackers m. m does not change the law of the score of one attacker. It controls how well we observe its distribution: the error of empirical probabilities, histograms and the KS distance is of order 1/√m. If instead the m attackers are pooled on the same n systems and their scores are added, the aggregate behaves like a walk of length mn: the total has variance mnσ² and the overall average has variance σ²/(mn), so n and m play symmetric roles in the aggregate average, while the spread across attackers (the histograms above) depends only on n.
  • Caveats. The model assumes independent, identically distributed systems. Real systems share software, configuration and vulnerabilities, so increments are positively correlated: the variance of Sn is then larger than nσ² and the CLT scaling can fail. The simulation is a best-case view of the aggregate risk.
