Homework 6

Statistical independence: contingency tables and the χ² test

Task

Given a bivariate dataset (X, F): build the contingency table programmatically, compute marginal and conditional frequencies, the expected frequencies under independence and the χ² statistic (and p-value); recall the theory of independence; interpret; and distinguish statistical association from causality.

Data. Main example: the Titanic passenger list (titanic.csv from the seaborn-data repository on GitHub, 891 passengers), X = passenger class (1st, 2nd, 3rd), F = outcome (died / survived). Two extra pairs are used for contrast: sex × survival (same data) and, from tips.csv (244 restaurant bills), sex of the payer × smoking section. The script reads the raw records and builds every table itself; scipy.stats.chi2_contingency is used only as a cross-check.

1. Theory

Independence of events. A and B are independent if P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B); when P(B) > 0 this is equivalent to P(A | B) = P(A): knowing B does not change the probability of A.

Independence of two discrete variables. X (r values) and F (c values) are independent iff for all pairs

P(X = xi, F = fj) = P(X = xi) · P(F = fj) ⇔ P(F = fj | X = xi) = P(F = fj) for every i

i.e. all conditional distributions of F given X coincide with the marginal one. With absolute counts Oij, row totals ni·, column totals n·j and total n, the marginal frequencies are ni·/n and n·j/n, and the conditional ones are Oij/ni· (row profiles) and Oij/n·j (column profiles).

Expected frequencies under independence. Replacing probabilities by the observed marginal frequencies:

Eij = n · (ni·/n) · (n·j/n) = ni· n·j / n

Pearson’s χ² statistic.

χ² = Σi,j (Oij − Eij)² / Eij

Under H0 (independence) and for large expected counts (rule of thumb: all Eij ≥ 5), χ² is approximately χ² distributed with (r−1)(c−1) degrees of freedom (rc cells, minus 1 for the total, minus (r−1)+(c−1) estimated marginal probabilities). The p-value is P(χ²d.o.f. ≥ χ²obs); a small p-value means data this far from independence would be very unlikely if H0 were true. As an effect size I report Cramér’s V = √(χ² / (n·min(r−1, c−1))) ∈ [0,1], because χ² grows with n and the p-value alone says nothing about how strong the association is.

2.4 Interpretation

Survival rates fall steadily with class: 63.0% (1st), 47.3% (2nd), 24.2% (3rd), against 38.4% overall. The χ² is 102.9 on 2 degrees of freedom, and p ≈ 4.5e-23, so independence is rejected at any usual level: class and survival are statistically dependent. The residuals show where: 1st-class survivors and 3rd-class deaths are over-represented, 3rd-class survivors and 1st-class deaths under-represented. The association is of moderate-to-strong size (V = 0.34).

2.5 Sanity check of the null distribution

To see what “independent” looks like, I shuffled the survival labels 5000 times (which destroys any link with class while keeping the margins) and recomputed χ² each time: the mean is 1.99 (a χ² with 2 d.o.f. has mean 2), the 95th percentile is 5.94 (theory: 5.99) and the maximum is 15.5. The observed value 102.9 is far outside: none of the 5000 permutations came close (fraction ≥ observed = 0, i.e. permutation p < 1/5000).

4. Association versus causality

  • What the test says. A significant χ² says the data are inconsistent with P(F | X) being the same for all classes. It does not say why, nor in which direction anything acts.
  • Confounding. Class is tied to other variables that affect survival. Sex is related to class here (female/male × class: χ² = 17.0, p = 2.1e-04; 3rd class is 60% of men but 46% of women), and survival depends strongly on sex (§3.1). Stratifying by sex shows the class–survival association does not vanish within either group:
    subsetχ²d.o.f.p-valueCramér’s VP(survived | 1st / 2nd / 3rd class)
    female81.921.7e-180.510.97 / 0.92 / 0.50
    male33.027.0e-080.240.37 / 0.16 / 0.14
    all passengers102.924.5e-230.340.63 / 0.47 / 0.24
    So sex alone does not explain it away, but this does not prove that class causes survival: age, cabin location, access to lifeboats, family groups and many unobserved factors are also entangled with class. Observational data of this kind cannot separate those mechanisms; that needs a causal model or an experiment.
  • Not causal even in the best case. Independence and dependence are symmetric statements (swapping X and F gives the same χ²), while causation has a direction. Survival does not cause class, and the table cannot tell us which way, if any, the influence runs without outside knowledge.
  • Significance is not importance. χ² scales with n: with very large samples tiny, practically irrelevant deviations become “significant”. That is why V is reported.
  • Failing to reject is not proving independence. For the tips example, p = 0.96 means no evidence of dependence in 244 bills; a weak association could still be hidden by the small sample.
  • A caveat on the p-value. The Titanic list is (nearly) the whole population of passengers, not a random sample, so the p-value is best read as a measure of how unlike a random reshuffling of labels the observed table is (the permutation check of §2.5), rather than as sampling error around a population value.
  • Independence also differs from “uncorrelated”: for numeric variables independence implies zero correlation, but zero correlation does not imply independence; the χ² test looks at the whole joint distribution of categories.

Code

Full script (reads the CSV files, builds all tables and figures) (hw7_independence.py)
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Homework 6 - Statistical independence: contingency table, marginal/conditional frequencies,
expected frequencies under independence, chi-square statistic and p-value.
Everything is computed from the raw (X, F) records with plain Python/NumPy;
scipy.stats.chi2_contingency is used only as a cross-check.
Data: seaborn-data titanic.csv and tips.csv (downloaded from GitHub into ../data).
"""
import csv, json, os
from collections import Counter
import numpy as np
from scipy.special import gammaincc
from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency
import matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
DATA, BUILD = os.path.join(HERE, "..", "data"), os.path.join(HERE, "..", "build")

def read(fn, xcol, fcol, drop_empty=True):
    with open(os.path.join(DATA, fn), newline="") as f:
        rows = [(r[xcol], r[fcol]) for r in csv.DictReader(f)]
    return [r for r in rows if r[0] != "" and r[1] != ""] if drop_empty else rows

def analyse(pairs, x_order=None, f_order=None):
    n = len(pairs)
    cnt = Counter(pairs)
    xs = x_order or sorted({p[0] for p in pairs}); fs = f_order or sorted({p[1] for p in pairs})
    O = np.array([[cnt[(x, f)] for f in fs] for x in xs], float)        # contingency table
    rows, cols = O.sum(1), O.sum(0)                                      # marginal absolute frequencies
    E = np.outer(rows, cols) / n                                         # expected under independence
    chi2 = ((O - E) ** 2 / E).sum()
    dof = (len(xs) - 1) * (len(fs) - 1)
    p = float(gammaincc(dof / 2, chi2 / 2))                              # chi-square survival function
    V = float(np.sqrt(chi2 / (n * min(len(xs) - 1, len(fs) - 1))))      # Cramer's V
    ref = chi2_contingency(O, correction=False)
    out = {"n": n, "x": xs, "f": fs, "O": O.tolist(), "rows": rows.tolist(), "cols": cols.tolist(),
           "joint_rel": (O / n).tolist(), "px": (rows / n).tolist(), "pf": (cols / n).tolist(),
           "f_given_x": (O / rows[:, None]).tolist(),           # P(F=f | X=x)  (row profiles)
           "x_given_f": (O / cols[None, :]).tolist(),           # P(X=x | F=f)  (column profiles)
           "E": E.tolist(), "chi2": float(chi2), "dof": dof, "p": p, "cramers_v": V,
           "resid": ((O - E) / np.sqrt(E)).tolist(), "contrib": ((O - E) ** 2 / E).tolist(),
           "min_E": float(E.min()),
           "check": {"chi2_scipy": float(ref[0]), "p_scipy": float(ref[1]), "dof_scipy": int(ref[2]),
                     "max_abs_diff_E": float(np.abs(ref[3] - E).max())}}
    if len(xs) == 2 and len(fs) == 2:
        yates = chi2_contingency(O, correction=True)
        out["yates"] = {"chi2": float(yates[0]), "p": float(yates[1])}
    return out

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- datasets
RES = {}
cls = {"1": "1st class", "2": "2nd class", "3": "3rd class"}
tit = [(cls[x], {"0": "died", "1": "survived"}[f]) for x, f in read("titanic.csv", "pclass", "survived")]
RES["A"] = analyse(tit, ["1st class", "2nd class", "3rd class"], ["died", "survived"])
RES["A"]["title"] = "Titanic: passenger class (X) × survival (F)"
tit_s = [(s, {"0": "died", "1": "survived"}[f]) for s, f in read("titanic.csv", "sex", "survived")]
RES["B"] = analyse(tit_s, ["female", "male"], ["died", "survived"])
RES["B"]["title"] = "Titanic: sex (X) × survival (F)"
tips = read("tips.csv", "sex", "smoker")
RES["C"] = analyse(tips, ["Female", "Male"], ["No", "Yes"])
RES["C"]["title"] = "Restaurant tips: sex of the payer (X) × smoking section (F)"
# stratification of A by sex (confounding check)
strat = {}
with open(os.path.join(DATA, "titanic.csv"), newline="") as f:
    allrows = list(csv.DictReader(f))
for sx in ("female", "male"):
    pr = [(cls[r["pclass"]], {"0": "died", "1": "survived"}[r["survived"]]) for r in allrows if r["sex"] == sx]
    strat[sx] = analyse(pr, ["1st class", "2nd class", "3rd class"], ["died", "survived"])
RES["strat"] = strat
# class distribution within sex (is sex related to class?)
RES["sex_class"] = analyse([(r["sex"], cls[r["pclass"]]) for r in allrows], ["female", "male"], ["1st class", "2nd class", "3rd class"])
# simulated independent data: to show what chi2 looks like when independence holds
rng = np.random.default_rng(7)
sim_x = rng.choice(["a", "b", "c"], 891, p=[.24, .21, .55]); sim_f = rng.choice(["0", "1"], 891, p=[.62, .38])
RES["sim"] = analyse(list(zip(sim_x, sim_f)), ["a", "b", "c"], ["0", "1"])
# Monte-Carlo check of the chi-square approximation: permute F, recompute chi2
Oa = np.array(RES["A"]["O"]); xa = np.array([tit[i][0] for i in range(len(tit))]); fa = np.array([t[1] for t in tit])
perm_chi = []
for _ in range(5000):
    fp = rng.permutation(fa)
    O = np.array([[np.sum((xa == x) & (fp == f)) for f in RES["A"]["f"]] for x in RES["A"]["x"]], float)
    E = np.outer(O.sum(1), O.sum(0)) / O.sum(); perm_chi.append(((O - E) ** 2 / E).sum())
perm_chi = np.array(perm_chi)
RES["perm"] = {"max": float(perm_chi.max()), "q95": float(np.quantile(perm_chi, .95)), "frac_ge_obs": float((perm_chi >= RES["A"]["chi2"]).mean()),
               "mean": float(perm_chi.mean()), "obs": RES["A"]["chi2"]}
json.dump(RES, open(os.path.join(BUILD, "results3.json"), "w"), indent=1)

# --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- figures
BG, TXT, MUTED, GRID = "#171e2e", "#e6eaf2", "#8d98b0", "#2a3550"
plt.rcParams.update({"figure.facecolor": BG, "axes.facecolor": BG, "savefig.facecolor": BG, "text.color": TXT, "axes.labelcolor": TXT,
    "xtick.color": MUTED, "ytick.color": MUTED, "axes.edgecolor": GRID, "grid.color": GRID, "font.size": 10, "axes.titlesize": 11})
COL = ["#e86f8c", "#6ee7a8"]

def fig_profiles(key, fn):
    R = RES[key]; fig, axs = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 4))
    gx = np.array(R["f_given_x"]); px = np.array(R["pf"])
    ax = axs[0]; bottom = np.zeros(len(R["x"]))
    for j, fl in enumerate(R["f"]):
        ax.bar(R["x"], gx[:, j], bottom=bottom, color=COL[j % 2], label=f"F = {fl}", width=.6)
        for i, v in enumerate(gx[:, j]): ax.text(i, bottom[i] + v / 2, f"{v:.2f}", ha="center", va="center", color="#0f1420", fontsize=10)
        bottom += gx[:, j]
    ax.set_title("Conditional frequencies P(F | X)"); ax.set_ylim(0, 1.18); ax.legend(loc="upper center", ncol=2, facecolor=BG, edgecolor=GRID)
    ax = axs[1]; res = np.array(R["resid"]); lim = max(2.5, np.abs(res).max())
    im = ax.imshow(res, cmap="RdBu_r", vmin=-lim, vmax=lim, aspect="auto")
    ax.set_xticks(range(len(R["f"])), R["f"]); ax.set_yticks(range(len(R["x"])), R["x"])
    for i in range(res.shape[0]):
        for j in range(res.shape[1]): ax.text(j, i, f"{res[i, j]:+.1f}", ha="center", va="center", color="#0f1420" if abs(res[i, j]) < lim * .6 else "white", fontsize=11)
    ax.set_title("Pearson residuals (O − E)/√E"); ax.grid(False)
    fig.tight_layout(); fig.savefig(os.path.join(BUILD, fn), dpi=140); plt.close(fig)

fig_profiles("A", "hw7_A.png"); fig_profiles("B", "hw7_B.png"); fig_profiles("C", "hw7_C.png")
# permutation distribution figure
from scipy.stats import chi2 as chi2d
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 3.8))
ax.hist(perm_chi, bins=60, density=True, color="#4da3ff", alpha=.7, label="χ² under H₀ (5000 label permutations)")
xx = np.linspace(0, perm_chi.max(), 300); ax.plot(xx, chi2d.pdf(xx, 2), color="#f2a65a", label="χ² density, 2 d.o.f.")
ax.set_xlim(0, 20); ax.annotate(f"observed χ² = {RES['A']['chi2']:.1f}  →\n(far beyond the axis)", xy=(19.9, .12), ha="right", color="#e86f8c")
ax.legend(facecolor=BG, edgecolor=GRID); ax.set_xlabel("χ²")
fig.tight_layout(); fig.savefig(os.path.join(BUILD, "hw7_perm.png"), dpi=140); plt.close(fig)
print("done")
